# Requests to remove a page from the Internet Archive

The Internet Archive's Wayback Machine is part of Western Friend's public
record. ADR 0005 makes archiving public pages the default, but a request about
a particular person or page deserves an individual editorial decision. A
change to our site and a removal from the Wayback Machine are separate
actions.

## Who decides

The Editor decides whether to change or unpublish a page and whether Western
Friend should ask the Internet Archive to remove or exclude it. Consider the
requester's connection to the person or material, the specific concern, and
whether correcting or limiting the information would address it. Consult the
website maintainer about technical changes. Escalate legal threats or urgent
safety concerns to the appropriate Western Friend leadership; don't make
promises about removal.

## Handling and recording a request

1. Acknowledge the request. Ask which page and archived copies they mean, and
   what they want changed, if that isn't clear. Be kind and don't promise an
   outcome.
2. The Editor reviews the request and decides what, if anything, to change on
   our site and whether to contact the Archive.
3. Record the date, page and snapshot URLs, the concern and requested action,
   the decision and reason, and any site changes or Archive correspondence
   and outcome in Western Friend's private editorial records. Keep only the
   personal information needed to handle the request, and don't put sensitive
   details in a public issue or on the page.
4. Tell the requester what Western Friend did and what remains outside our
   control.

## Requesting action from the Internet Archive

When the Editor decides to proceed, use the Internet Archive's
[Help Center](https://help.archive.org/) or email **info@archive.org**. Give
the exact live page URL and each known Wayback snapshot URL. State whether
Western Friend is asking for existing snapshots to be removed, future
captures to be excluded, or both, briefly explain the request, and ask the
Archive to confirm what action it can take. Keep its reply with the editorial
record.

The Archive decides whether to honor a request and controls its own process.
Changing or unpublishing our page does not delete existing snapshots; a
request is not a guarantee that the Archive will remove them or prevent
future captures. Other copies, caches, or search results may remain, too.
Don't change site-wide crawler settings or `robots.txt` for an individual
request without a separate decision by the Editor and website maintainer.

## Choosing what to do on our site

- **Edit** when a correction or removal of specific details addresses the
  concern. Publish the correction and check the live page.
- **Unpublish** when the page should no longer be publicly available on
  Western Friend. This does not remove archived snapshots or copies held
  elsewhere.
- **Noindex** only when the page should remain publicly accessible at its URL
  but should not appear in search-engine results. A `noindex` instruction
  neither hides a page from visitors nor removes or prevents Wayback captures,
  and search engines decide when to reflect it. There is no editor-controlled
  per-page `noindex` setting at the time of writing; involve the website
  maintainer before offering this option.

## What to tell the requester

Explain the decision and any change Western Friend made. If we contacted the
Archive, say that its response is separate and not guaranteed. Be clear that
removing or changing the live page cannot by itself erase archived snapshots,
other copies, or search results.
